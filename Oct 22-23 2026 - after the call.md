# 22–23 Oct 2026: after the call

## The call
On 22 Oct I spoke to someone I like for about an hour and a half. She was honest and kind. She doesn't feel the same way, and she won't give me hope, because hope would only hurt me more. She told me not to depend on one factor for my happiness. I said "correct, I accept that." She was right.

## What I said that I want to keep
- "I depend on you for my entire stability." Reading this back, that's too much weight to put on one person.
- "These 16 minutes are the only time I felt alive in two or three weeks."
- "I thought I had no wishes. I do, I forgot them. The well went missing." (இங்க ஒரு பெரிய கிணறு இருந்துச்சு, கிணத்தை காணோம்.)
- "I really want to change."
- "I'm really proud of myself that I've come this far alive."

## What I realised the next morning
It may not be love. She's an anchor I assigned myself so I could feel like a normal guy who is in love, because when you don't desire anything, you start asking why you're living at all. The anchor isn't the problem. Having only one anchor, one that keeps shaking, is the problem. So I'll build more anchors, ones I control. The first one is Oddtables, Sunday dinners with strangers.

## A pattern I'm owning
When something heavy lands on me, I can't hold it alone, so I tell someone, even when it isn't mine to tell. I did that this week and I regret it. My dad is the same way. New rule before every story: **is this mine to tell?** If it isn't, it goes to my therapist or my diary, nowhere else.

## The rule I gave someone else, applied to me
If a person admits a mistake, regrets it and apologises, they should be forgiven. That includes me.

— paartha
